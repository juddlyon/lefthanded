<h1 class="post-title">Famous left-handed people: 70+ iconic southpaws</h1>

<p><strong>Who are the most famous left-handed people?</strong> Around 10% of the global population is left-handed — roughly 800 million people — and that 10% includes an outsized share of US presidents, Renaissance painters, rock-and-roll guitarists, Olympic athletes, and Hollywood stars. Names that show up on almost every list: Leonardo da Vinci, Isaac Newton, Barack Obama, Paul McCartney, Oprah Winfrey, Jimi Hendrix, Lady Gaga, Babe Ruth, and Prince William.</p>

<h2 id="musicians">Famous left-handed musicians</h2>

<p>Music is one of the few fields where being left-handed visibly changes the craft, because right-handed instruments dominate. Some lefties play flipped, some restring, and a few — Paul McCartney, Hendrix — became iconic for it.</p>

<li><strong>Paul McCartney</strong> — the most famous left-handed bassist ever; played a left-handed Höfner violin bass throughout the Beatles era.</li>

<li><strong>David Bowie</strong> — left-handed in life but right-handed on guitar.</li>
<li><strong>Ringo Starr</strong> — naturally left-handed but plays a right-handed drum kit, which is widely credited as the source of his unusual style.</li>
<li><strong>Phil Collins</strong> — left-handed drummer playing a right-handed kit; another stylistic-by-accident lefty.</li>
<li><strong>Lady Gaga</strong> — left-handed pianist and singer.</li>
<li><strong>Justin Bieber</strong> — left-handed; tried to play guitar left-handed but eventually learned right-handed.</li>

<li><strong>Cardi B</strong> — left-handed.</li>

<h2 id="gender">What gender is mostly left-handed?</h2>

<p><strong>Men are more often left-handed than women.</strong> Across large meta-analyses, men are about 1.23 times more likely to be left-handed — roughly 12% of men compared to 10% of women. The gap is consistent across cultures and has shown up in samples covering more than two million participants. The reason is debated; prenatal testosterone exposure is one leading hypothesis. We cover the data in detail in <a href="/left-handed-women-statistics/">how rare left-handed women are</a>.</p>

<p>The famous-people lists reflect this — men outnumber women among historical lefty greats, partly because of the underlying gender gap and partly because women have been systematically underrepresented in the public record.</p>

<h2 id="adhd">Is left-handedness linked to ADHD?</h2>

<p><strong>Slightly, but the link is small.</strong> A 2018 meta-analysis found that ADHD diagnoses are modestly more common among left- and mixed-handed people — around 17 to 20 percent of ADHD patients are non-right-handed, compared to the general-population rate of about 10 to 12 percent. Most left-handers do not have ADHD. The same weak association shows up for autism and dyslexia, and likely reflects shared neurodevelopmental factors rather than handedness causing the conditions. We cover the full picture in <a href="/left-handedness-and-adhd/">left-handedness and ADHD</a>.</p>

<h2 id="why-this-many">Why are there so many famous left-handed people?</h2>

<p>There aren't, statistically. Left-handers make up about 10% of any large group of accomplished people — Nobel laureates, Mensa members, PhDs, professional athletes — which matches their share of the general population almost exactly. The exception is interactive sports (boxing, tennis, baseball) and US presidents from the modern era, where the rates are noticeably higher.</p>

<p>The reason famous lefties feel overrepresented is memory bias. When a Hollywood star or an emperor is left-handed, it stands out and gets noted. Right-handed celebrities are the unmarked default, so their handedness is invisible. We unpack the IQ and creativity claims in <a href="/are-left-handed-people-smarter/">are left-handed people smarter</a> and <a href="/are-left-handed-people-more-creative/">are left-handed people more creative</a>.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3 id="faq-most-famous">Who is the most famous left-handed person?</h3>

<p>By global recognition, the leading candidates are Leonardo da Vinci, Barack Obama, and Paul McCartney. Da Vinci is probably the single most cited example because of his mirror writing and Renaissance-spanning influence, but Obama is the most recognizable living lefty.</p>

<h3 id="faq-percentage">What percentage of famous people are left-handed?</h3>

<p>Roughly the same as the general population — about 10%. The notable exceptions are US presidents (5 of the last 9), elite tennis players (15–20%), and elite boxers (20–30%). In most other fields, the percentage of left-handers tracks the population baseline closely.</p>

<h3 id="faq-bible">What does the Bible say about left-handed people?</h3>

<p>The Bible mentions left-handed people positively in the Book of Judges — Ehud, a left-handed Israelite judge, used his hand to conceal a dagger and assassinate the Moabite king Eglon. The tribe of Benjamin is described as having 700 elite left-handed slingers. The negative connotations of "left" in Western culture come more from later Greek and Latin tradition than from the Bible itself. More in <a href="/left-handed-in-bible/">left-handed people in the Bible</a>.</p>

<h3 id="faq-criminals">Are most criminals left-handed?</h3>

<p>No. The popular myth that serial killers are disproportionately left-handed is not supported by data. Some famous criminals were left-handed — Jack the Ripper has been speculated to be, the Boston Strangler was, John Dillinger was — but the rate among convicted criminals tracks the 10% population baseline. The myth persists because of the historical association of "left" with "sinister" (the Latin word for "left").</p>

<h3 id="faq-how-known">How do we know which historical figures were left-handed?</h3>

<p>For modern figures, photographs and direct accounts. For pre-photographic figures, evidence comes from a mix of sources: contemporary biographer notes (Vasari on Michelangelo and Raphael), analysis of artistic technique (chisel-mark direction, brush-stroke patterns), surviving handwriting samples, and in rare cases, mirror writing (Leonardo da Vinci). For some commonly listed historical lefties — Napoleon, Joan of Arc — the evidence is genuinely contested.</p>
